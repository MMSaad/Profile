About

Architecture-first, vendor-second

My work sits at the intersection of software engineering and cloud strategy — architecting backend services, designing distributed systems, and implementing the practices that keep them running in production.

I care about building things that don't just work on launch day, but scale gracefully and stay maintainable as they grow. That shows up in how I pick platforms: I design and deploy across both AWS and Google Cloud, choosing the right services for the problem rather than forcing a single vendor.

Most of what I build lives under extreme seasonal load. Hajj and Umrah platforms sit idle for months and then absorb national-scale traffic in a matter of days — which makes serverless-first architecture, elastic data layers and real observability design requirements rather than nice-to-haves.

The harder problems are rarely the compute ones. Integrating with government providers whose API contracts shift underneath you, keeping data flowing with zero loss, and shipping one platform as several white-labelled products — that's where the architecture actually earns its keep.

I also write. Tech, Unfiltered is my biweekly newsletter on code, cloud and architecture — real solutions and lessons from a decade of building software.
